### Analysis of Pollution Transport Mechanisms

This section investigates the physical mechanisms driving PM2.5 fluctuations, specifically validating the hypothesis that "North winds clean, while South winds accumulate." Using a Flexdashboard framework, we visualized the relationships between wind direction, wind speed, and pollution intensity to isolate the drivers of air quality.

**Spatial Distribution (Pollution Rose)**

![O42zZa](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/AllenYGY/ImageSpace@main/uPic/O42zZa.png)

The analysis begins with a radial visualization of pollution frequency across 16 wind directions. The data reveals a distinct spatial dichotomy:

- **Northwest Sector:** Dominated by "Green" (Good) and "Gold" (Moderate) air quality events, confirming the mountains as a source of clean air.
- **Southeast Sector:** Characterized by a high frequency of "Purple" (Unhealthy/Hazardous) events. This confirms the baseline observation that pollution is directionally dependent, accumulating significantly when winds blow from the industrialized South and East.

**Seasonal Dynamics (Winter vs. Summer)**

![OViZox](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/AllenYGY/ImageSpace@main/uPic/OViZox.png)
Disaggregating the data by season reveals that the "North cleans, South accumulates" mechanism is highly season-dependent.

- **Winter:** The contrast is most severe. Southern winds in winter are associated with heavy hazardous pollution, likely due to the combination of regional heating emissions and stable atmospheric conditions.
- **Summer:** The pattern flattens significantly. The directional penalty for Southern winds disappears due to unstable air and better vertical mixing, indicating that wind direction is a critical predictor primarily during the heating season.

**The Physical Driver: Wind Speed vs. Direction**
![lUqWw1](https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/gh/AllenYGY/ImageSpace@main/uPic/lUqWw1.png)
To understand *why* North winds are cleaner, we analyzed Mean PM2.5 levels color-coded by wind speed. This visualization uncovered the governing physical mechanism:

- **Dispersion (North):** The cleanest sectors (NW) correspond to the highest wind speeds (bright yellow in the visualization). This indicates that North winds physically disperse smog through high-velocity air movement.
- **Stagnation (South/East):** The most polluted sectors (SE/E) correspond to the lowest wind speeds (dark purple). This suggests that pollution accumulation in the South is driven by stagnant air masses that fail to flush out contaminants.

**North-South Linkage (Lag Analysis)**
Finally, a temporal lag analysis comparing "North Yesterday" vs. "South Today" shows that most data points lie above the identity line. This indicates an accumulation effect: even when pollution tracks from North to South, concentrations tend to increase by the time the air mass settles in the South, reinforcing the finding that the Southern region acts as a sink for pollutants due to stagnation.
